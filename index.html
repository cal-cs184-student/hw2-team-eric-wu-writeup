Section I: Bezier Curves and Surfaces

Part 1: Bezier curves with 1D de Casteljau subdivision

In this part, de Casteljau's algorithm is used to evaluate a Bezier curve. The algorithm works by doing linear interpolation along adjacent pairs of a list of N control points, to generate a list of N-1 control points. This is then repeated until you are left with only one point, which is a point along the Bezier curve.

To generate all points along the curve, the algorithm is applied with a varied parameter for the linear interpolation.

Part 2: Bezier surfaces with separable 1D de Casteljau

When we can evaluate a Bezier curve, this gives us a way to extend de Casteljau's algorithm to evaluate a points on a Bezier surface. A point on a N x N Bezier surface can be evaluated by first evaluating each row of points, to get N points along each row. This evaluation is done with a parameter u. Then, a separate parameter t is used, treating these N points as the control points of a bezier curve. This finally gives us a point at (u, v) on the Bezier surface.

Section II: Triangle Meshes and Half-Edge Data Structure

Part 3: Area-weighted vertex normals

This section asks us to implement area-weighted vertex normals. I implemented weighting by area by simply looping through all adjacent half edges of the vertex, and collecting the positions of neighbor vertices of the target vertex. Then, I would take the cross product of adjacent neighbors, and keep track of the sum of all cross products.

Recall that the cross product's magnitude is proportional to the area spanned by the parallelogram enclosed by the two vectors, which means that it is also proportional to the area of the face (assuming all faces are triangles). This means we only need to normalize the sum at the end, to get an area-weighted sum of all normals of all faces.
